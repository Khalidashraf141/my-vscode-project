# **Affective Domain – Defend the Correctness of the Following Statements**

These answers focus on **attitudes, values, perceptions, and emotional acceptance**, as required in the *Affective Domain*.

---

## **(a) “A C++ stream is a file.”**

From a learner’s perspective, treating a stream *as if it were a file* creates a sense of **simplicity and comfort**.  
It helps students appreciate that all forms of input and output can be handled uniformly.  
This emotional acceptance builds confidence and reduces fear of dealing with different I/O devices.  
Thus, the statement feels correct because it promotes a **consistent and reassuring mental model**.

---

## **(b) “C++ never truncates data.”**

Students naturally develop **trust** in C++ because the language demands explicit instructions for modifying or narrowing data.  
This creates the emotional belief that C++ protects their information unless they intentionally change it.  
Such trust encourages a careful and disciplined attitude toward programming.  
Therefore, the statement reflects a **positive emotional conviction** about C++'s strict handling of data.

---

## **(c) “The main advantage of width() is that we can use one width specification for more than one item.”**

Learners appreciate tools that make formatting simpler.  
The ability to reuse a single width setting gives them a sense of **efficiency, neatness, and convenience**.  
This leads to a favorable attitude toward using formatting functions.  
Hence, the statement is supported because students value **less repetition and cleaner output**.

---

## **(d) “The get() function provides single-character input that does not skip white spaces.”**

Many students struggle when input skips spaces and newlines.  
Knowing that `get()` reads characters *exactly as typed* gives them a sense of **predictability and control**.  
This reduces confusion and builds trust in the function.  
Thus, learners emotionally accept the correctness of the statement due to its **clarity and precision**.

---

## **(e) “The header file <iomanip> can be used in place of <iostream>.”**

Beginners often feel that `<iomanip>` seems to enhance or extend `<iostream>`.  
This impression may emotionally lead them to believe it can replace `<iostream>`.  
The statement reflects a learner’s **perception** that formatting power comes from `<iomanip>`, creating a positive attitude toward using it.  
Thus, the correctness is defended on the basis of **perceived usefulness**, not strict technicality.

---

## **(f) “We cannot use both C I/O functions and C++ I/O functions in the same program.”**

Students prefer **uniformity and clarity** in their code.  
Mixing two different styles (like `printf` with `cout`) can feel confusing or disorganized to them.  
Emotionally, they believe sticking to one system ensures neatness and avoids errors.  
Hence, the statement is defended based on a desire for **clean, consistent programming practice**.

---

## **(g) “A programmer can define a manipulator that could represent a set of format functions.”**

The idea of creating custom manipulators appeals to a student’s sense of **creativity, control, and ownership**.  
It gives them confidence that they can extend the language’s capabilities.  
This fosters a positive attitude toward reusable, organized formatting.  
Thus, students defend the statement because it reflects **personal empowerment and mastery** over C++ formatting tools.
